Genetic Algorithm

🐍 Python 🧬 Evolutionary Computation 📈 Scientific Computing 🕸️ Gene Regulatory Networks

Overview

This project explores the behavior of genetic algorithms through implementations from scratch, computational experiments, and comparisons with established optimization frameworks. The goal is to understand how evolutionary operators and population structure influence optimization and the emergence of complex solutions.

The experiments investigate relationships between fitness, mutation, selection, and population diversity, as well as the application of evolutionary optimization to gene regulatory networks. The figures below present the experimental results, with each section describing the question being investigated and the interpretation of the resulting data.

Research Questions

  • How do evolutionary parameters influence convergence and population diversity?
  • How does a custom genetic algorithm compare with established optimization implementations?
  • How can evolutionary processes discover gene regulatory networks capable of producing target spatial expression patterns?

Experimental Results

Each figure examines a different set up parameters and their effect on the epochal behavior of the evolutionary dynamics. Epochal behavior is characterized by extended periods without change in fitness, followed by faster jumps. N is the gene count. K is the gene length. The total genome length is constant so N*K = 60. M is the size the population. q is the muation rate. Each graph plots the number of iterations against the average fitness in orange and the best fitness in blue. The parameters from the the first graph will be altered one by one in the following graphs.

Discussion

Taken together, these experiments provide insights into how the relationships between these fundamental evolutionary parameters effect the epochal behavior. The first experiment uses parameters for the canonical example of epochal evolution, which are modified in three seperate paths to see their impact.

Firstly, Figures 2 and 7 modify the population size from the canonical example. In Figure 2, increasing the population size generally smooths out the dynamics, while Figure 7 illustrates how decreasing population causes noisy dynamics. Population size therefor has the characteristic of controlling the stochasticity, where more members greater approximate the average behavior and less are more suseptible to stochastic noise. However, epochal behavior is still present, even if noisy. Next, experiments 3, 5, and 8 modify one parameter from experiments 2, 1, and 7 respectively, each of which disrupt the epochal behavior. In figure 3, the gene length is decreased from experiment 2, which speeds up the discovery of correct genes faster than epochs can be maintained causing monotonic increase. In figure 5 and 8, the mutation rates of the canonical example and experiment 7 are increased, creating too much noise to sustain epochs. A high mutation rate causes correct genes to be mutated faster than they can be spread among the population. In contrast to the noisy dynamics of a low population size, a high mutation rate breaks epochal behavior. Finally, experiments 4, 6, and 9 change one parameter from the previous experiments to return epochal behavior. Experiment 4 decreases the population from experiment 3, which slows down the discovery of correct genes and brings back epochs from monotonic growth. Furthermore, Experiment 6 increases the population from experiment 5, returning epochal behavior from the noisy chaos due to the increased mutation rate. This examplifies how a balance of population size and mutation rate characterize healthy epochal evoltuion, as they both control the noise. Finally, Experiment 9 increases the gene length to return epochs, creating a larger barier to increase fitness. Since fitness increases so slowly, the selection is enough to keep the best fitness despite the incredibly noisy dynamics. However, the point of this research is in the quantitative relationships between these parameters, and so such qualitative explanations are merely hypotheses on the observations within these figures to help guide the research.
